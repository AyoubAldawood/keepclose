# keepclose

**A mobile app that helps international students mitigate homesickness and loneliness by supporting routine-based calls with family and friends.**

Regular calls with family and friends help international students cope with homesickness and loneliness, but time zones, study demands and daily responsibilities make those calls hard to keep up. To address this, I surveyed 50 international students, designed and prototyped keepclose in Figma, and evaluated it through scenario-based walkthroughs with 10 students. All ten completed every journey with no failed steps, and the design's restraint (no streaks, scores or automatic tracking) drew the strongest endorsement.

## Design Approach

The project followed a human-centered design process, anchored in Peters' (2023) wellbeing-supportive design guidelines: support autonomy, and prefer low pressure to pressure. They shaped what was designed, what was deliberately avoided, and how it was evaluated (whether the design *felt* supportive, not only whether tasks could be completed).

Key design moves:

- **A layer, not a replacement.** keepclose shows whom to call and when, then hands the call to the apps students already use, such as WhatsApp and FaceTime.
- **Quick to call.** “Call now” is one tap from opening keepclose, so calling is almost as quick as messaging.
- **Deliberate restraint.** No streaks, scores, badges or comparisons between contacts, and nothing is read from the phone.
- **Calm look and tone.** Cream backgrounds, one muted green, soft amber instead of red, and gentle wording throughout.
- **Gentle prompts, not nagging.** Gentle reminders were the most wanted feature, yet too many reminders was the top deterrent, both at 74%. So instead of frequent notifications, anyone not called within the student's calling goal moves to the top of Contacts, marked by a soft amber dot.
